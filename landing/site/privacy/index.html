Privacy

Privacy in Folkbranch: where your family’s data lives and who sees it

A family archive holds the most personal things a family has. Folkbranch keeps it in one SQLite file and a folder of photos on your own computer or server, hides the living from readers on the server, sends nothing to us, and asks before its assistant writes anything.

Where your data lives

On your computer or your server, in one SQLite database and a folder of photos. Nothing is sent to us. The server copies the database each day, and you can download a full copy at any time under Settings → Data and backups. Only people you invite can see anything: the archive is closed to search engines and photos need a sign-in. If you have not installed it yet, see the install guide.

No telemetry, and the one update check

Folkbranch has no telemetry. The one call the server makes by itself asks GitHub whether a newer release exists. It runs at most twice a day and sends nothing about your archive. To turn it off, set FOLKBRANCH_UPDATE_CHECK=off in .env; FOLKBRANCH_SELF_UPDATE=off only hides the Update now button.

Other things leave your server only because you choose them:

  • Connecting an AI provider sends what the assistant reads to that provider, and the app says so first.
  • Cloud speech, if the owner connects it, sends each recording to that service, which keeps nothing of it.
  • A model for faces or speech downloads to the browser only when you press its button. Faces are found and matched on your device.
  • The family map comes with the app and asks no other server. The map on the Places screen comes from OpenStreetMap once a device allows it, which then sees the part of the world on screen, not names or dates.

How the living are kept private

Readers, guests and read-only agents never see living relatives, or anyone and any story you choose to keep private. The server enforces it, not the page. Addresses and phones of the living are for editors only. A reader’s GEDCOM has no record, not even an unnamed one, for someone they may not see, and the file you can give relatives leaves the living out. A person who died, or a single story, can also be kept from readers, and someone marked private in an imported GEDCOM stays hidden.

Roles: owner, editor, reader

  • Owner. Sets the archive up and holds its keys: the owner makes access tokens, connects the AI provider and a speech service, and sees the notice when a new release is out.
  • Editor. Edits the archive, including people, pages, photos and recordings, and sees the addresses and phones of the living.
  • Reader. Sees the tree labelled from where they stand (“your grandfather”, “your aunt”), without the living, and suggests corrections for you to accept.

Relatives join with a link, with no password to remember; after that they sign in with Yandex ID, VK ID, Telegram, Google or a code by mail, whichever you switch on. A view-only link can have a password and an expiry date. A story link lets one person record and nothing more: they see no one else in the family, and what they send waits for your review.

The assistant asks before it writes

The optional assistant in the app is off by default. It acts as the person talking to it, with their role. Ordinary edits ask first, or can be allowed for one conversation; deleting, importing, undoing and settings always ask. Each answer’s edits can be undone with one button and show in history as “Assistant → name”.

The same rule holds for agents you connect from outside over MCP. A read-only token sees what a reader sees, with the living hidden. An editor token’s writes wait in a draft that you read line by line, accept or reject. Page text is written by relatives, but a model may take it for commands, which is why review is on by default. Every change is in the history and can be rolled back.

A copy for relatives

Relatives can get one file with the chart, cards and photos. It opens in any browser, with no server and no internet, and the living stay hidden. A full copy of the archive for yourself is separate and has everything. Moving to another program is covered in the GEDCOM guide, and how Folkbranch compares with hosted services in the comparison.

Questions

Does Folkbranch collect data about me or my family?

No telemetry. The one call the server makes by itself asks GitHub whether a newer release exists, at most twice a day and with nothing about your archive. Setting FOLKBRANCH_UPDATE_CHECK=off stops it.

Can search engines find my family tree?

No. The archive is closed to search engines, photos need a sign-in, and only people you invite can see anything.

Who can see living relatives?

Not readers, guests or read-only agents: the server hides them, not just the page. Addresses and phones of the living are for editors only.

Does the AI assistant see my family’s data?

The assistant is off until the owner adds a key. Once on, what it reads in the archive is sent to the provider you picked, or stays home with a local model. Ordinary edits ask first, and deleting, importing, undoing and settings always ask.
